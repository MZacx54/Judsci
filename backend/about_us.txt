Established as the policy-making, coordinating and supervisory body for the Diocese’s social development programs, the Commission operates under the direct authority of the Bishop.The organization started in 2003 and is registered with Corporate Affairs Commission of Nigeria (CAC) in 2016 with the name: Justice Development and Social Cohesion Initiative (JUDSCI) with registration number CAC/IT. No. 90258. JDPC works with communities across Bauchi and Gombe States, reaching all people irrespective of religion, ethnicity, or political affiliation, with particular focus on vulnerable and marginalized groups. Guided by its vision of a just and peaceful society and its mission of promoting human development, good governance, and interreligious tolerance, the Commission implements interventions across WASH, peace building, good governance, sustainable agriculture, women and youth empowerment, and prison ministry. Over the years, the Commission has built strong partnerships with government institutions, civil society organizations, and faith-based actors to advance social cohesion and community resilience.OUR VISIONTo build a just and peaceful society where unity, human dignity, and respect for life are upheld, and people are empowered with hope and joy in all creation.OUR MISSIONWe are a faith-based organization committed to promoting integral human development through capacity building, good governance, and collaboration that fosters social and religious tolerance.OUR CORE VALUESWe love, care and have respect for human dignity irrespective of belief.We cherish unity and peaceful co-existence among all.We value accountability and transparency based on responsibility entrusted to us.We value the promotion of equality and quality services.We cherish the protection of the environment.We value teamwork to empower communities towards self-reliance and just society.We value the protection of life.We value education, rural/urban development and quality health services.IMPACT DATA Total Beneficiaries: 35,000 householdsCommunities Reached (currently served): 60 communities; Families struggle with limited access to clean water, decent sanitation, and reliable sources of income. For women and children in particular, the burden is heavy, walking long distances for water, coping with poor hygiene conditions, and living with the constant risk of waterborne diseases.The situation is made worse by a changing climate. Rains no longer come as expected, dry seasons last longer, and the land does not produce as it once did. Many smallholder farmers, already vulnerable, are losing their main source of food and income. These challenges are not just about survival, they touch on the dignity of people who want nothing more than to live healthy, peaceful lives and provide for their families.At the same time, the use of firewood for cooking continues to damage the environment and puts women and girls at risk of respiratory illness and physical harm. There is a growing need for safer, cleaner, and more sustainable alternatives like briquettes, simple changes that can make a big difference in daily life.Despite these challenges, there is strong community spirit and a desire for change. People want to rebuild trust, strengthen local leadership, and work together toward solutions. Access to clean water and sanitation gives families the chance to live in good health. And small steps toward environmental care, like using briquettes, are acts of justice, both for people and for the land.This project is grounded in the reality of the communities it serves. It builds on what people already know and do, supporting them with tools, training, and structures that promote lasting change.
